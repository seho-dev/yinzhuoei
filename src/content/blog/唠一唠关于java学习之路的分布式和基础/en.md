I finished a Vue frontend project and shipped it. I learned a great deal along the way. Today, a conversation with my mentor turned to work.

Some background: associate degrees and two years of study. My mentor referred two friends to a small company run by an acquaintance. They needed two “coders” to build a backend administration system in a few months, without particularly demanding technical requirements—basic CRUD using SSM would do. Yet apparently, after a month of interviewing new graduates and bootcamp graduates, they had not found a single person who could implement CRUD.

My friends had a chance to test themselves, so I went along. It was supposed to be a three-hour session, but they were politely shown the door during the second hour. I think there were a few reasons:

1. Nerves: it was their first coding test in this kind of setting.
As far as I know, both friends have solid coding skills. Never mind fancy technologies such as distributed systems, threading, or circuit breakers—they are certainly very capable when it comes to CRUD.
2. Too much confidence in their skills.
Our school, especially our class, focuses on hands-on practice. Over two years, we have built more demos and practice projects than I can count. They were overconfident, did not prepare seriously, and underestimated the opportunity. I am sure they will learn from it.

**Which Matters More: Fundamentals or Advanced Technology?**

My view is that a deep understanding of Java fundamentals is essential. The fancy technologies are things you pick up through outside sources: other people teaching you, videos, or exposure at work.

So, for new graduates preparing for spring recruitment: do not chase every fashionable technology, and do not neglect the basics. Do not let any single demo or practice exercise give you false confidence.
