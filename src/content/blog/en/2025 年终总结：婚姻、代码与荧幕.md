## Marriage

In 2025, we finally wrapped up everything wedding-related: the trip, the reception to thank our family and friends, and the wedding photos. Nothing left on the list. We plan to visit Hong Kong in about a month. Neither my wife nor I has ever been to a Disney park, so we're really looking forward to Hong Kong Disneyland.

This first year of marriage has made me even more certain that **I don't want children**.

There are several reasons. Neither of our families can give the next generation a better starting point, and our own education and understanding of the world aren't exceptional either. More importantly, we're both quite honest about being "self-interested": we'd rather spend our money on food and fun than invest it in raising a child. Pets and travel give us all the emotional fulfillment we need.

Our parents don't support this decision, but having children is ultimately a decision for our generation to make, based on our own considerations.

What I mean is that perhaps we'll change our minds someday, if we're financially comfortable, three generations can work together to support a child, and our parenting approach and understanding can provide the environment that child needs to grow. But for now, I sincerely want to say this to my readers: **If you want to have a child, first think carefully about whether you can give them a good life in the world they'll inherit.**

Society will only become more polarized: either you belong to an elite at the very top, or you end up stuck in low-level service work with little prospect of getting ahead. The population is shrinking, jobs are disappearing, and competition will become many times fiercer than it is now. This isn't pessimism for its own sake. It's a reality already unfolding.

Fortunately, my wife and I are completely on the same page about this. That's what gives me the most peace of mind.

## Open-Source Projects

Toward the end of last year, I started a new open-source project called **Bud**.

**Bud** is plugin infrastructure aimed at giving software developers a unified plugin development experience. It lets plugin authors write [WebAssembly](https://webassembly.org/) / [JavaScript](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/JavaScript) programs, making it easy for host applications to add plugin support.

**Bud** itself is laying the groundwork for another open-source project of mine, tentatively called **RayChat**.

**RayChat** is not another AI wrapper.

There are already more than enough of those. Since [OpenAI](https://openai.com)'s models took off, chat-based personal [AI Agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_agent) applications have sprung up everywhere. But most of these open-source projects focus on agent orchestration itself, rather than on the act of chatting.

**RayChat** aims to solve a different problem: the burden of keeping up with conversations.

The core idea is to aggregate messages from channels such as WeChat, Telegram, Slack, and Discord into a unified API, through reverse-engineered instrumentation or API integrations, then orchestrate those messages in **RayChat**. Agents become part of the [Workflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workflow), continually collecting message content and archiving it in a database. You can then make full use of local models to analyze and process your own messages. Your AI agent is no longer just a tool that passively answers questions, but a partner sitting at the top of your information stream with a genuine understanding of your communications as a whole.

So **RayChat** is both a multichannel chat orchestration application and an AI workflow application. It lets you handle messages from every channel in one place, free from ecosystem lock-in.

**Bud** provides the plugin infrastructure behind it. Plugins can decouple the message-processing logic for individual channels and handle rich content such as images and multimedia.

Both projects are still in their early stages. I'll keep sharing updates as they progress.

## Games

Once again, few new games really caught my interest this year. I still spent most of my gaming time on QQ Speed and CrossFire. A friend recommended Delta Force, but after downloading it I found myself completely lost. The learning curve was a little too steep, so I gave up.

I also tried Split Fiction this year. It's a great game for two people to play together, and we had a really good time. As for mobile games, after trying so many, Honor of Kings is still the one I keep coming back to.
