Having a blog is a wonderful thing. It is your own little corner of the world, shared with friends from the tech community. Although mine has had a few hundred thousand visits since it started, the day-to-day readership is actually quite small, so I often use it to share what is on my mind.

When my family got its first computer during my middle-school years, I spent a lot of time gaming. I admit that games had a real impact on me and indirectly contributed to my poor performance on the high-school entrance exam. But those years were not just about games; there was programming, too. I built my first website, which more or less marked the start of my little entrepreneurial journey, and met plenty of people in tech who were a few years older than me. Some of them may not even be in this industry anymore. Between running a website with all sorts of things going on and my love of gaming, my schoolwork inevitably fell behind, and I ended up at a pretty poor high school. Unexpectedly, though, my little career got a second wind. Hahaha.

That is why I always say connections matter most. Drawing on the network I had built, I got involved in three startup projects during high school. I also began to truly appreciate good code and wanted to push myself further. Then I graduated early and came to Xi'an to formally continue my studies. I am incredibly grateful to my teachers, He and Le, for teaching me backend development over those two years. I also met a great group of friends. My old roommates are still working in all sorts of roles at major internet companies or as independent developers. They are genuinely impressive people.

I am 21 now, and next year will mark my third year in the industry. Maybe my perspective is limited, but with the effort I put in every day, I think I should be doing reasonably well among developers with three years of experience. Still, this is not a field where you can let your guard down. You really have to study hard every day, as though the postgraduate entrance exam were just around the corner. Only then will you have something to show for a year of effort and a chance to get ahead. For a stretch this year, I was studying so hard that I barely knew day from night. When my mom came to Xi'an for a visit, I got up after five in the morning to start studying, only for her to order me straight back to bed…

Whether you were born in the 1980s, 1990s, or 2000s, if you work in the internet industry, you need to approach every day as though your job could disappear tomorrow. Keep learning and improving—technically, in product thinking, in operations, or even in communication. Learn something every day rather than coming home, flopping onto the bed, and firing up a game to that TiMi jingle (assuming you do not have children to look after). Keep doing that for long enough, and you really will stagnate.

This is not pointless competition for its own sake. People see how I live and sometimes say, “Stop studying! Why not just take it easy? Doesn't looking at code every day make you sick?”

What I want to say is that if you genuinely love this work, it will not. I have met very experienced engineers in their thirties who are still hungry to learn new technologies, and I really admire them. A day's work has not worn down their drive. Even with children and families, they still make time to learn. That is an attitude. That is passion.

As for dating—hahaha—my grandma has been nudging me about finding someone this year. I honestly do not want a relationship, yet I also really want one. Maybe I am not suited to dating: I have my own plans for every day, and the thought of someone suddenly disrupting them makes me quite anxious. Maybe I am afraid of relationships. My second-oldest sister got together with a friend I have known for ten years, and honestly, they are really sweet together. Just thinking about it has me shedding another lonely single-person tear. I will let things happen naturally. By the way, my oldest sister is still single. Neither of my sisters is the type to sacrifice everything for a younger brother, so you guys can relax. If anything, I am the one who spoils his sisters. Hehe.

Anyway, the new year is almost here, and I write a year-end review every year. We can save the technical stuff for that.
