# A Budget Guide to a Wireless, Minimalist Desk with Pinduoduo

As the title suggests, this isn't a sponsored post. We martial artists have to uphold our code of honor, you know.

![1758.jpg](http://static.yinzhuoei.com/typecho/2020/11/12/95355758455805.jpg)

I mainly needed to add more monitors for upcoming work, so I looked at lots of impressive desk setups online. They all seemed to feature a MacBook Pro, a Switch, keyboards costing thousands of yuan, monitors costing over ¥4,000, and monitor lights approaching ¥1,000. Good grief—I couldn't afford that even if I sold myself. If you're a junior developer without much money but still want a practical desk, this is the guide for you!

Now for the last Pinduoduo purchase. There's a reason I've saved it for last: this was a real shopping horror story, though it worked out in the end. My budget meant dropping from a 4K monitor to a 2K model, which also halved the price. I decided to take a chance on a 2K monitor from Pinduoduo: a 24-inch 2K display for a little over ¥500.

When it arrived, the narrow bezels looked great, there were no dead pixels, and it had all the ports I needed. The only problem? It wouldn't turn on. Come on, seller—where's your sense of fair play? Why send me a broken one? After troubleshooting with customer service, we found that the power adapter was faulty. The shop sent a replacement, and the monitor powered up. Just as I thought I could finally enjoy my bargain, I found that it wouldn't connect to my laptop. More troubleshooting revealed a faulty HDMI port. Unfortunately, because I had already mounted it, the seller said it could no longer be resold and wouldn't accept a return. I had to spend ¥70 on shipping to send it back for repair. After the repair, it powered up and worked. Finally!

Buying this monitor on Pinduoduo was a bumpy ride, but at least it ended well. Hopefully my experience serves as a warning.
