## Introduction

I write a year-end review every year so that, three or five years down the road, I can look back through the blog and see my growth at a glance. There is something deeply satisfying about that. This year has been especially rewarding: I reached a number of firsts in my career. Perhaps I felt a little lost during my first few years in the industry. Although I now have high hopes for the next few years, reaching my ultimate goal will take far more time and effort. I will come back to that goal later. In previous years, my reviews focused on three themes: the pandemic, personal growth, and technical growth. This year, I want to add a few more sections and upload some photos to preserve these valuable memories.

Put on a song and listen while you read.

## The Pandemic

Just as the year was coming to an end, Xi'an suffered an outbreak second only to the one in Wuhan. I have no idea whether I will be able to go home for Chinese New Year. I am currently working from home, which is very comfortable, but there is one major downside: my body clock is completely out of sync. I go to bed at 11 p.m. and wake up naturally at 8 a.m. On a normal workday, I would be up at six or even earlier. Still, it is not a huge problem. Without the commute, I have plenty of time to study despite sleeping longer. Xi'an's handling of the outbreak has repeatedly made the trending topics: baffling decisions by the authorities, the One-Code Pass mini program crashing about once a week, and no contingency plan. It has been a real embarrassment.

A week after the city went into lockdown, we finally received vegetables from the government: white radishes, Chinese cabbage, potatoes, and onions. No idea when the next delivery will arrive...

One experience worth mentioning is a mini program I built with a few friends: a map showing nearby COVID testing sites. Traffic surged after launch. It appeared in print media and on the trending topics, and we were interviewed. It was an unforgettable experience.

Later, once residential communities were placed under stay-at-home quarantine, Xi'an's temporary testing sites disappeared. The program helped many people for a while. It may no longer be useful, but it had its moment...

Working from home has been great, especially since I am staying with my older sisters. I can write code in my room without worrying about meals; all I have to do is wash the dishes, haha. All in all, life at home during the lockdown has been quite comfortable. Of course, comfort is relative. You have probably heard about people in Xi'an fainting from hunger, someone being detained for going out to buy steamed buns, a woman miscarrying outside a hospital because of testing requirements, and an elderly father dying of heart disease because he could not get medical care in time. Compared with them, I really have been lucky.

## Plans for the Next Few Years

Friends who know me will know that my salary after graduation was very, very low: at one point, just RMB 1,200 a month, barely enough to support myself. Two years later, it is more than ten times that. It sounds pretty good, but it is still far below where I want to be. 2022 will be my third year in the industry. The night before writing this post, a conversation with my friend Xiao Ma changed some of my original plans:

![WX20220105-152052.png](https://static.yinzhuoei.com/typecho/2022/01/05/672691739705448/WX20220105-152052.png)

I have decided to make 2022 a year of stability. There is already so much on the agenda, including seemingly endless exams and getting my driver's license. I would rather get through the year steadily than chase the immediate thrill of a supposedly high-paying RMB 15,000–16,000 offer. I want 2022 to be a highly productive year, with rapid improvement in my skills, so I can build a solid foundation for what comes next.

Personally, I hope Zhigui will be the last company I work for in China for the next few years. The people here are very nice. Once the pandemic is over—a few years from now—and assuming my undergraduate studies and IELTS preparation go smoothly, I will most likely apply for a part-time master's program abroad. That would fulfill a long-held educational dream and offer a way out of the rat race. I also hope to continue my career in another country.

Nontechnical goals for 2022:

- Get through all my exams, make good progress with English, and pass my driving test.

## On the Technical Side

To be honest, I did not produce many open-source projects this year; most of them only got started in the second half. Vite's popularity signals a sea change in frontend build tooling. Webpack still has its uses, but most new small and medium-sized projects will choose Vite. Its arrival has also helped popularize newer tools such as esbuild. Languages like Go and Rust can use their strengths to make compilation, checking, and related tasks faster and more reliable.

After joining a new team in the second half of the year, I started working on templates and a new technology stack, and open-sourced a set of templates and a project management tool.

I also wrote an article introducing several of the templates: [Vite-Based Templates](https://www.yinzhuoei.com/index.php/archives/570/).

I did not make any major technical breakthroughs this year, but I gained new insights into Rust and TypeScript, which I will share in future articles. The team has Flutter work planned for next year, so I will write a few short posts about that too.
